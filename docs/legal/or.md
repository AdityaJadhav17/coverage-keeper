# Oregon (OHP)

Last reviewed: 2026-11-08. All sources retrieved 2026-11-08.

Statute text comes from the official ORS chapter pages at oregonlegislature.gov, 2025 edition. Each chapter page notes that the 2026 session amended or added sections. We opened the four 2026 session laws listed for ch. 646A (chs. 11, 17, 85 and 96). None amends ORS 646A.570 to 646A.589 or 646A.600 to 646A.628. We did not check the 2026 amendment tables for the other chapters.

## Summary

- **The Oregon Consumer Privacy Act (OCPA) is the biggest Oregon question.** It has covered nonprofits since July 1, 2025, at 100,000+ Oregon consumers a year. If we cross that line, health and immigration data are "sensitive data" and need opt-in consent first. Pass-through counts as processing. Whether we reach 100,000 is a traffic fact. See [Topic 3](#topic-3-general-consumer-privacy).
- **The AG can sue without a cure period since January 1, 2026,** for up to $7,500 per OCPA violation. There is no private right of action.
- **The breach law (ORS 646A.600 to 646A.628) likely applies at any size.** It covers nonprofits and volunteer activities, and anyone who "processes" or "possesses" personal information. See [Topic 4](#topic-4-data-breach-notification).
- **The practice-of-law line is the main open advice question.** OSB Formal Op. 2005-137 says self-help software with no human contact is not law practice, but it assumes stored content. Our AI writes a fresh summary for each letter. See [Topic 1](#topic-1-unauthorized-practice-of-law).
- **Deception law (UTPA) reaches our own claims.** ORS 646.607(12) covers websites that describe data handling and then act differently. ORS 646.608 bars implying an affiliation we lack. See [Topic 7](#topic-7-government-affiliation) and [Topic 11](#topic-11-consumer-protection).

## Details

### Topic 1: Unauthorized practice of law

[ORS 9.160(1)](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors009.html) bars practicing law unless the person "is an active licensee of the Oregon State Bar". The working line comes from _Oregon State Bar v. Gilchrist_, 272 Or 552 (1975), as summarized in [OSB Formal Ethics Op. 2005-137](https://www.osbar.org/_docs/ethics/2005-137.pdf) (rev. 2016). That opinion says self-help software "simply is not the practice of law, unauthorized or otherwise." It assumes "previously stored legal information". In _Gilchrist_, explaining how forms fit a person's situation was law practice. Our AI writes a new summary of each person's letter, which is a new fact pattern.

We did not open _Gilchrist_ itself. Justia returned 403 and is unofficial. The quote comes through the OSB opinion.

**What we must do:**

- Never say whether someone qualifies. The guardrail does this today.
- Keep the screening result as "This may apply to you. Ask OHP."
- Say that no human or lawyer reviews the output and that it is not legal advice.
- Never fill in or choose forms for the person.
- Give legal-aid referrals.

**Status leaning:** likely does not apply, pending the lawyer.

### Topic 2: Health data privacy

We found no Oregon law like Washington's My Health My Data Act. Health data falls under the OCPA as sensitive data ([ORS 646A.570(18)](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors646A.html)). Letters and screening answers reveal "mental or physical condition or diagnosis". Some reveal "citizenship or immigration status".

If the OCPA applies (Topic 3):

- A controller may not "Process sensitive data about a consumer without first obtaining the consumer's consent" (646A.578(2)(b)). The [DOJ FAQ](https://www.doj.state.or.us/consumer-protection/for-businesses/privacy-law-faqs-for-nonprofits/) agrees.
- Consent must be an affirmative act. Inaction or a dark pattern is not consent (646A.570(6)).
- Revoking consent must be as easy as giving it (646A.578(1)(d)).
- We need a written data protection assessment, kept 5 years (646A.586).

ORS 646A.572(3)(g) says the Act doesn't stop a controller from "providing a product or service that a consumer specifically requests". It might cover processing a letter the user chose to upload. The controller must prove the exemption (646A.572(7)), and the DOJ FAQ doesn't mention it. A lawyer decides.

**What we must do if the OCPA applies:** add an opt-in consent step before upload, make revocation easy, and write the assessment. Keep screening answers in the browser.

### Topic 3: General consumer privacy

**Coverage** ([ORS 646A.572(1)](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors646A.html)). The Act applies to anyone serving Oregon residents who, in a year, controls or processes personal data of 100,000+ consumers, or 25,000+ while earning 25%+ of revenue from selling data. The 2025 edition has no general nonprofit exclusion. The [DOJ nonprofit FAQ](https://www.doj.state.or.us/consumer-protection/for-businesses/privacy-law-faqs-for-nonprofits/) says "The law takes effect for nonprofits on July 1, 2025." Public bodies are excluded, so OHA is out and we are in.

**Pass-through counts.** "Processing" includes "collecting, using, storing, disclosing" (646A.570(14)). "Personal data" includes a unique identifier linked to a device (646A.570(13)), which may reach the IP address we hold for a minute.

**Duties if covered:**

- **Privacy notice (646A.578(4)):** data categories including sensitive data; purposes; how to use rights and appeal; categories shared with third parties and what kind of entity each is; a monitored email; our legal name as registered with the Secretary of State; how to submit requests.
- **Rights (646A.574, .576):** access, correction, deletion and opt-out, with 45 days to respond and an appeal.
- **Data minimization (646A.578(1)(b))** and safeguards equal to ORS 646A.622 (646A.578(1)(c)).
- **Processor contract (646A.581(2)):** instructions, purpose, data type and duration, confidentiality, deletion or return at the end, compliance information, and flow-down to subprocessors. Anthropic's 30-day retention has to fit "delete ... at the controller's direction".
- **Opt-out signals (646A.578(5)):** we do no targeted ads or sales, so these matter little. A lawyer should say whether we still need a statement.

**Enforcement.** Only the AG enforces (646A.589(7)). Penalty up to $7,500 per violation (646A.589(4)), with a 5-year limit. The [DOJ business FAQ](https://www.doj.state.or.us/consumer-protection/for-businesses/privacy-law-faqs-for-businesses/) confirms the AG no longer has to offer a cure period from January 1, 2026 (see the 2025 c.417 §5 note).

**What we must do:**

1. Decide whether to comply now. That is cheaper than tracking the threshold.
2. If we comply: OCPA privacy notice, sensitive-data consent, data protection assessment, processor terms with Anthropic and Vercel, monitored privacy email.
3. If we don't: count unique Oregon users each year.

### Topic 4: Data breach notification

[ORS 646A.600 to 646A.628](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors646A.html):

- A "covered entity" is anyone who "owns, licenses, maintains, stores, manages, collects, processes, acquires or otherwise possesses personal information" in business or "volunteer activities" (646A.602(5)).
- "Personal information" includes a name with "any information about a consumer's medical history or mental or physical condition", or a name with a health insurance ID plus another identifier (646A.602(12)).
- The definitions don't exempt data in transit. But a breach means unauthorized acquisition of data a person "maintains or possesses". A lawyer should say how brief possession in memory fits.
- Anthropic is likely our "vendor". A vendor must tell us within 10 days of finding a breach, and tell the AG above 250 consumers if we haven't (646A.604(2)).
- A breach-law violation is also a UTPA violation (646.607(9)).

**What we must do:**

1. Write a breach plan: notify consumers within 45 days, and the AG above 250 people (646A.604(1), (3)).
2. Build a written security program with administrative, technical and physical safeguards, including vendor safeguard contracts (646A.622(2)(d)).
3. Add breach-notice clauses to the Anthropic and Vercel terms.

### Topic 5: AI-specific laws

SB 1546 ([enrolled](https://olis.oregonlegislature.gov/liz/2026R1/Downloads/MeasureDocument/SB1546/Enrolled); [overview](https://olis.oregonlegislature.gov/liz/2026R1/Measures/Overview/SB1546); [Or. Laws 2026 ch. 85](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/lawsstatutes/2026orLaw0085.pdf)) covers AI companions designed to simulate a sustained, human-like relationship. It excludes software operating "solely for the purpose of customer service or support, ... information analysis". Coverage Keeper is stateless and reads one letter. The law has a private right of action ($1,000 per violation or actual damages), so drifting into scope carries real risk.

The enrolled text has no effective-date section. The January 1, 2027 date and the March 31, 2026 signing come from secondary sources. **Unconfirmed.** We found no Oregon law on AI in health communications. We did not open the DOJ generative-AI guidance under the OCPA.

**What we must do:** stay stateless and task-limited. Re-check before adding memory or open chat. Label output as AI-generated.

### Topic 6: Medicaid and benefits assistance

The [OHA Community Partner Requirements](https://www.oregon.gov/oha/HSD/OHP/Documents/CP-Requirements.pdf) (updated 1/7/2021) say certification lets people get an Assister ID and access the ONE eligibility system. It requires a contract, background check and training. We don't fill out or submit applications, use ONE or act for members. We found no Oregon ban on helping without certification and no payment ban that matters for a free service. [ORS 413.201](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors413.html) sets up OHA outreach grants we don't take. OAR 410-120-0045 was blocked (**Unconfirmed**).

**What we must do:** never call ourselves an "OHP-certified Community Partner" or "assister". Point users to certified partners and OHP Customer Service. If we later help people apply, look at the Volunteer Organization Agreement.

### Topic 7: Government affiliation

[ORS 646.608(1)(c)](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors646.html) bars causing "likelihood of confusion or of misunderstanding as to affiliation, connection, or association with, or certification by, another." 646.608(1)(e) bars claiming an affiliation we don't have. The UTPA reaches nonprofits as "persons". The open question is whether we act "in the course of the person's business, vocation or occupation" while charging nothing.

We found no OHA rule on outside use of "Oregon Health Plan" or "OHP". We did not search USPTO (**Unconfirmed**) or open the ORS ch. 162 impersonation statutes.

**What we must do:**

- On every page, in both languages: "Coverage Keeper is run by [nonprofit]. We are not the Oregon Health Authority or OHP."
- No state seal or OHA logo.
- Label phone numbers as "OHP Customer Service (official)".
- Link only to oregon.gov sources.

### Topic 8: Immigration consultant laws

[ORS 9.280(1)](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors009.html) bars acting as an immigration consultant "for compensation". An "immigration matter" is a proceeding, filing or action affecting immigration or citizenship status, or an action of DHS, DOJ, State or Labor. A state Medicaid letter is not one. A violation counts as a violation of ORS 9.160. Last amended 2025 c.32 §26.

**What we must do:**

- Explain only what the Medicaid letter says.
- Never advise on status, USCIS documents or immigration consequences. Refer "public charge" questions out.
- Add: "We can't give immigration advice. For immigration questions, talk to a licensed immigration lawyer or a DOJ-accredited representative."
- Re-check "for compensation" if donations ever tie to service.

### Topic 9: Accessibility

[ORS 659A.400(1)(a)](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors659A.html) defines a place of public accommodation as "Any place or service offering to the public accommodations, advantages, facilities or privileges". A free public website could be a "service". 659A.142(4) bars disability discrimination there. We found no Oregon statute or case on websites. 659A.142(5), on state government, does not apply to us.

**What we must do:** meet WCAG 2.1 AA and publish an accessibility contact.

### Topic 10: Language access

[ORS 413.559(1)](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors413.html) says "a health care provider shall work with a health care interpreter from the health care interpreter registry". We are not a provider and take no OHA grant (ORS 413.201).

**What we must do:** get a fluent Spanish review. A wrong Spanish deadline feeds UTPA risk. If the OCPA applies, its notice must be "reasonably accessible", so publish the privacy notice in Spanish.

### Topic 11: Consumer protection

[ORS 646.607(12)](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors646.html) covers a website "related to the person's business" that describes how data is used, kept or deleted, when practice is "materially inconsistent with the person's statement or representation." Private suits (646.638) need an "ascertainable loss of money or property" and a willful violation, so private risk is low for a free tool. The AG and district attorneys can still seek injunctions, and violating one costs up to $25,000 per violation (646.642). Whether a free nonprofit acts "in the course of" business is open.

**What we must do:**

- Make every privacy claim exactly true: "We don't save your letter. Anthropic may keep it up to 30 days."
- No accuracy guarantees. Say: "AI can make mistakes. Check the date on your letter."

### Topic 12: Children

Under the OCPA, a "child" is under 13 (646A.570(5)), and a child's data is sensitive data. 646A.578(2)(b)-(d) requires COPPA-style handling and bans targeted ads, profiling and sale for people under 16. These bite only if the OCPA applies and we know a user is a child. The work rule targets adults 19-64. We did not search for a separate Oregon children's online privacy law.

**What we must do:** say the tool is for adults. Don't add age tracking. Letters may name children in the household; that data falls under the consent step if the OCPA applies.

### Topic 13: Charitable solicitation

[ORS 128.620(1)](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors128.html) says "the solicitation of funds for charitable purposes in this state shall constitute doing business therein." A charitable organization that receives property must register with the AG (128.660). No exemption in 128.640 fits. We take no donations today.

**What we must do before taking donations:** register with the Oregon DOJ Charitable Activities Section, file annual reports, and check fundraiser rules if we hire one. We did not open ORS 128.670, 128.801 to 128.898 or OAR ch. 137 div. 010.

### Topic 14: Other

- **Doctor note ([ORS 677.085](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors677.html)), likely does not apply.** The practice of medicine includes offering "to diagnose, cure or treat in any manner". The template asks the person's own doctor whether they can write a statement. Keep it neutral, never suggest a diagnosis, and keep "Dr." out of the product name. We found no Oregon rule on soliciting medical certifications.
- **Data broker registration ([ORS 646A.593](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors646A.html)), does not apply.** Brokered data is data organized "for sale or licensing". We sell nothing.
- **OCPA data protection assessment (646A.586):** see Topic 2.

### Start-point corrections

- **OCPA:** the range is ORS 646A.570 to 646A.589. The cure period ended January 1, 2026.
- **ORS 9.160:** the heading is now "Bar license required to practice law" ("active licensee").
- **ORS 9.280:** reaches only paid work on federal immigration matters, framed as a violation of ORS 9.160.
- **UTPA:** add 646.607(12) (website privacy statements) and 646.607(9) (breach-law violations).
- **OHP name:** no OHA name-use rule found. The hook is UTPA 646.608(1)(c) and (e).
- **Missing from the start points:** SB 1546 and ORS ch. 128.

## Questions for a lawyer

1. Does an AI summary of one person's own Medicaid letter, with no human contact and no eligibility call, stay on the "not practice of law" side of OSB Formal Op. 2005-137, which assumes "previously stored" information?
2. For the OCPA's 100,000-consumer threshold, do we count every Oregon visitor whose IP we hold for a minute, or only people who upload a letter?
3. Does ORS 646A.572(3)(g) ("a product or service that a consumer specifically requests") let us process sensitive data in an uploaded letter without separate opt-in consent?
4. Do Anthropic's commercial terms and DPA meet ORS 646A.581(2)? Does 30-day retention fit "delete ... at the controller's direction"?
5. Does brief in-memory possession make us a covered entity that "maintains or possesses" data under the breach law? Is Anthropic our "vendor"?
6. Does a free nonprofit act "in the course of the person's business, vocation or occupation" under ORS 646.607 and 646.608?
7. Is a public website a "place of public accommodation" under ORS 659A.400 and 659A.142?
8. Is our "not OHA / not OHP" disclaimer enough, or should we ask OHA to review our use of the OHP name?
9. Does SB 1546 (2026 ch. 85) reach a stateless letter reader? When does it take effect?
10. Once we take donations, must we register under ORS 128.660 before the first Oregon gift or before the first solicitation?
