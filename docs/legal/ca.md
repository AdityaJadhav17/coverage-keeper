# California (Medi-Cal)

Last reviewed: 2026-11-09. Sources retrieved 2026-11-08 or 2026-11-09 as marked in [Sources](#sources).

leginfo.legislature.ca.gov returns 403 to scripted downloads, so we read every code section in a browser. Some long sections we read only in part; the [Sources](#sources) note lists which. DHCS pages render with JavaScript, so we also read those in a browser.

## Summary

- **The breach law (Civ. Code §1798.82) covers nonprofits.** Medical and health insurance information count as personal information. Notice is due within 30 calendar days. Whether a pass-through tool "owns or licenses" the data is for a lawyer; Anthropic's 30-day retention makes it live. See [Topic 4](#topic-4-data-breach-notification).
- **AB 489 (B&P §4999.9) applies as a wording limit.** Our AI output must never imply that a licensed health professional is giving the advice. AB 3030, SB 1001, SB 243 and the AI Transparency Act do not reach us. See [Topic 5](#topic-5-ai-specific-laws).
- **B&P §17533.6 bars a private group from implying a government connection.** We need a clear "not affiliated with DHCS or the State of California" line and no state look-alike design. Asking for letter uploads may count as soliciting "information", which would trigger the statute's all-caps disclaimer. See [Topic 7](#topic-7-government-affiliation).
- **The UCL and False Advertising Law apply to our claims.** Neither exempts nonprofits or free services. "We store nothing" must sit next to Anthropic's 30-day retention. See [Topic 11](#topic-11-consumer-protection).
- **CCPA does not reach a nonprofit. CMIA likely does not reach a no-storage tool.** A lawyer should confirm CMIA, since §56.06(b) covers some consumer software. See [Topic 2](#topic-2-health-data-privacy).
- **The Unruh Act's damages ($4,000 minimum per violation) make WCAG 2.1 AA worth building to,** even though website coverage is unclear. See [Topic 9](#topic-9-accessibility).

## Details

### Topic 1: Unauthorized practice of law

[B&P §6125](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=BPC&sectionNum=6125) says "No person shall practice law in California unless the person is an active licensee of the State Bar." The statute doesn't define "practice law". We did not open the case law that does.

The legal document assistant (LDA) rules help draw the line:

- [§6400(d)(2)](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=BPC&sectionNum=6400) treats "general published factual information that has been written or approved by an attorney" as self-help that does not need LDA registration.
- The LDA rules apply only "for compensation". [§6401(c)](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=BPC&sectionNum=6401) exempts nonprofit staff who help "free of charge".
- §6400(g) forbids even registered LDAs from advising on rights, remedies or which option to choose. That is where an AI summary could slip.

**What we must do:**

- Keep the eligibility guardrail.
- Also block advice on appeal and fair-hearing strategy and on "which option you should choose".
- Have an attorney review the fixed text: screening questions, "ways to meet" and the doctor note. That fits the §6400(d)(2) framing.
- Add: "Not legal advice. Contact Medi-Cal or a legal aid office."

### Topic 2: Health data privacy

The Confidentiality of Medical Information Act binds providers, plans, contractors and businesses that [§56.06](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=56.06) "deems" providers.

- §56.06(b) covers software "designed to maintain medical information in order to make the information available to an individual", or for diagnosis or treatment. We store nothing and manage no records or treatment.
- [§56.05(j)](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=56.05) limits "medical information" to information from a provider, health care service plan, pharmaceutical company or contractor. DHCS and counties are not on that list. A Medi-Cal managed-care plan is a "health care service plan" (§56.05(g)), so plan letters are closer.
- §56.05(j)(2) treats immigration status known to a provider as medical information.
- §56.06(d) covers mental health digital services. We don't market any.
- We read [§56.10](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=56.10) and [§56.101](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=56.101) only in part.

**What we must do:** nothing required if counsel agrees. Keep the no-storage design, confirm Anthropic retention and ZDR, and don't add features that save letters or health history. Saving them would move us toward §56.06(b).

### Topic 3: General consumer privacy

**CCPA (does not apply).** [§1798.140(d)(1)](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=1798.140) defines "business" as an entity "organized or operated for the profit or financial benefit of its shareholders or other owners". A nonprofit is outside unless a for-profit controls it and shares its branding (d)(2), or it opts in (d)(4). The [AG FAQ](https://oag.ca.gov/privacy/ccpa) says the CCPA "generally does not apply to nonprofit organizations". There is no separate nonprofit exemption clause; the for-profit definition does the work.

**CalOPPA (unclear).** [§22575](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=BPC&sectionNum=22575) requires an "operator of a commercial Web site or online service" collecting PII from Californians to post a privacy policy. [§22577(a)](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=BPC&sectionNum=22577) limits PII to information "maintained by the operator in an accessible form". We maintain nothing and the site is free.

**What we must do:** meet §22575(b) anyway. The notice must list PII categories and third parties (Anthropic, Vercel), say how users learn of material changes, give an effective date, and say how we respond to "do not track" and whether others track users across sites. Link it from the homepage with the word "privacy" (§22577(b)).

**Confidence:** high on CCPA; medium on CalOPPA.

### Topic 4: Data breach notification

- [§1798.80(a)](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=1798.80) covers any business "whether or not organized to operate at a profit".
- [§1798.82(h)(1)(D)-(E)](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=1798.82) list medical information and health insurance information (including a subscriber ID, §1798.82(i)(3)) as personal information when paired with a name.
- Duties attach to whoever "owns or licenses" the data (§1798.82(a)). A party that "maintains" data it doesn't own must notify the owner immediately (§1798.82(b)). Whether we "own or license" data that only passes through memory is unsettled. It matters most for Anthropic's copies and any Vercel logs.

**What we must do:**

- Write an incident plan. Notify affected Californians "within 30 calendar days of discovery or notification of the data breach" (§1798.82(a)(2)(A)), under the "Notice of Data Breach" title with the set headings (§1798.82(d)).
- Above 500 Californians, send the AG a sample copy within 15 days of notifying people (§1798.82(f); [AG reporting page](https://oag.ca.gov/privacy/databreach/reporting)).
- Put breach-notice duties in the Anthropic and Vercel terms.
- Keep logs free of letter content.

### Topic 5: AI-specific laws

- **AB 489 (applies).** [§4999.9(b)-(c)](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=BPC&sectionNum=4999.9) makes health-professional title rules enforceable against "a person or entity who develops or deploys a system or device" whose wording implies advice comes from a licensed person. Effective 2026-01-01. That covers us as a deployer.
- **AB 3030 (does not apply).** [H&S §1339.75(a)](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=HSC&sectionNum=1339.75) binds "A health facility, clinic, physician's office, or office of a group practice". We are none.
- **SB 1001 (likely does not apply).** [§17941(a)](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=BPC&sectionNum=17941) covers bots that mislead to push a sale or a vote. We do neither. "Bot" means "an automated online account" ([§17940(a)](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=BPC&sectionNum=17940)).
- **SB 243 (likely does not apply).** [§22601](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=BPC&sectionNum=22601) covers companion chatbots that sustain a relationship. It excludes bots used only for "productivity and analysis related to source information" (§22601(b)(2)(A)).
- **AI Transparency Act, SB 942 (likely does not apply).** [§22757.1](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=BPC&sectionNum=22757.1) binds a "covered provider" that creates the AI system. That is Anthropic. We read the text only in part; the user threshold is **Unconfirmed**.

**What we must do:** add prompt and output rules so the reader never calls itself a nurse, doctor, counselor, case worker or similar, and never implies a licensed person reviewed the output. Check the Spanish for the same terms. Say plainly that AI wrote the summary; it costs little and backs up Topic 11.

### Topic 6: Medicaid and benefits assistance

The [Westlaw search listing](https://govt.westlaw.com/calregs/Search/Results?t_querytext=%22certified%20enrollment%20counselor%22&t_Method=tnc) places 10 CCR §6650 et seq. in "Chapter 12. California Health Benefit Exchange, Article 8. Enrollment Assistance". Headings include a counselor application (§6657), roles (§6664) and conflict-of-interest standards (§6666). Every section page returned HTTP 500, so the text is **Unconfirmed**. These are Covered California rules, not Medi-Cal assister rules. We did not open any Medi-Cal assister rules in the Welfare and Institutions Code.

**What we must do:** never call the tool or staff an "enrollment counselor", "certified", "navigator" or "assister". Don't fill out or submit applications. Have counsel read §6650-6670 before a certified partner embeds the tool.

### Topic 7: Government affiliation

[§17533.6(a)](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=BPC&sectionNum=17533.6) bars a nongovernmental entity from using any seal, name, term, symbol or content that "reasonably could be interpreted" as implying a government "connection, approval, or endorsement", including on a website. Factual references to Medi-Cal are likely fine. Design or naming that suggests DHCS runs the site is the risk.

§17533.6(c)(2) covers solicitations, including soliciting "information" or contributions. A covered solicitation must carry, on every page: "THIS PRODUCT OR SERVICE HAS NOT BEEN APPROVED OR ENDORSED BY ANY GOVERNMENTAL AGENCY, AND THIS OFFER IS NOT BEING MADE BY AN AGENCY OF THE GOVERNMENT." Asking users to upload a letter is arguably soliciting information. A lawyer decides.

The [DHCS Conditions of Use](https://www.dhcs.ca.gov/conditions-of-use/) treat most site content as public domain but warn that some, such as photographs, needs permission. We found no DHCS rule on the "Medi-Cal" name. Trademark status is **Unconfirmed**.

**What we must do:**

- On every page in English and Spanish: "Coverage Keeper is run by [nonprofit]. It is not part of, approved by, or endorsed by DHCS, Medi-Cal, or the State of California."
- No state or DHCS logos, seals or look-alike colors.
- Keep "Medi-Cal" out of the product name and domain.
- Consider the full §17533.6 disclaimer on upload and donation pages, pending counsel.
- Don't copy DHCS photos or graphics.

### Topic 8: Immigration consultant laws

[§22440](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=BPC&sectionNum=22440) covers acting as an immigration consultant "for compensation". [§22441(c)](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=BPC&sectionNum=22441) defines compensation as "money, property, or anything else of value". An immigration matter (§22441(b)) is a proceeding or filing "affecting the immigration or citizenship status of any person". Explaining how status affects Medi-Cal is a benefits question.

One trap: consultant activities include "Making referrals to persons who could undertake legal representation activities" (§22441(a)(5)). Donations or partner payments could later be argued to be compensation. The [State Bar](https://www.calbar.ca.gov/public/concerns-about-attorney/avoid-legal-services-fraud/avoid-fraud-immigration-consultants) says consultants may not advertise as "notario" or "notario publico" (compare [§22442.3](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=BPC&sectionNum=22442.3)).

**What we must do:**

- Stay free.
- Never describe the tool or org as "notario", "notario público", "abogado" or "licensed".
- Block advice on immigration forms, status, applications, or whether Medi-Cal affects immigration status.
- Point those questions to "an immigration attorney or a DOJ-accredited representative" with no paid referrals.
- Have a fluent speaker check the Spanish for these terms.

### Topic 9: Accessibility

- [Civ. §51(b)](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=51) guarantees equal services "in all business establishments of every kind whatsoever". §51(f) makes any ADA violation an Unruh violation.
- [§52(a)](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=52) sets damages "in no case less than four thousand dollars ($4,000)" plus fees.
- Open: is a free nonprofit website a "business establishment", and does a site with no physical place count? Appellate cases exist but we did not open them. **Unconfirmed.**
- [Gov. §11135](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=GOV&sectionNum=11135) covers programs that receive "any financial assistance from the state". It does not apply until we take state money.

**What we must do:** build to WCAG 2.1 AA (text output for letter images, labeled forms, contrast, keyboard use, screen-reader checks in both languages). Publish an accessibility statement with a contact. Re-check §11135 before any state grant.

### Topic 11: Consumer protection

The UCL ([§17200](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=BPC&sectionNum=17200)) bans "any unlawful, unfair or fraudulent business act or practice". The FAL ([§17500](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=BPC&sectionNum=17500)) covers misleading statements by "any person, firm, corporation or association", "including over the Internet". Neither exempts nonprofits or free services. We did not check case law on whether a free service is a "business act". A §17533.6 violation can also be an "unlawful" UCL act.

**What we must do:**

- Put "Anthropic may keep your upload up to 30 days" next to "we store nothing", unless ZDR is signed. Mention Vercel analytics and the one-minute IP hold.
- Don't say "accurate" or "guaranteed". Say the summary can be wrong and to check the letter or call Medi-Cal.
- Keep the "not an eligibility decision" statement.

### Topic 12: Children

[§22580](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=BPC&sectionNum=22580) limits marketing listed products to minors. We don't advertise. The Age-Appropriate Design Code ([§1798.99.31(a)](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=1798.99.31)) binds "A business that provides an online service, product, or feature likely to be accessed by children". We did not open §1798.99.30, so whether "business" is for-profit only is **Unconfirmed**. Its current enforceability is also **Unconfirmed**. The screening asks about age, so minors may use the tool.

**What we must do:** nothing required. Say the site is for adults or a parent helping a child, and keep collecting nothing.

### Topic 13: Charitable solicitation

[§12582.1](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=GOV&sectionNum=12582.1) covers California charities and "any similar foreign corporation doing business or holding property in this State". [§12585(a)](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=GOV&sectionNum=12585) requires registration with the AG "within 30 days after the corporation, unincorporated association, or trustee initially receives property". The [AG](https://oag.ca.gov/charities/initial-reg) says assets include "public donations, property, government grants". No [§12583](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=GOV&sectionNum=12583) exemption fits.

**What we must do:** decide where to incorporate. If in California or doing business here, register within 30 days of the first gift or grant and renew yearly. Check B&P §17510 et seq. (solicitation disclosures, not opened) before donation drives.
